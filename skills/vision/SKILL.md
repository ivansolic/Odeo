# Product vision

The vision is the **shared picture of the future** you are trying to create, the north
star that outlives any single feature. Grounded in modern product-vision practice: a
good vision is inspiring, persistent (2 to 5 year horizon), and about the customer's
better future, not your roadmap.

## When to use
- Starting a new product or a major strategic bet.
- When the team is busy but directionless, lots of features, no shared "why."

## Process
1. **Who and what better future:** for which customers does the world get meaningfully
   better, and how? Describe their life *after* you succeed.
2. **The change you're betting on:** the shift in technology, behavior, or market that
   makes this possible and urgent.
3. **Draft the vision narrative:** a short, vivid story of that future (a few paragraphs,
   not a slogan). Customer-centric, not feature-centric.
4. **Stress-test it:** is it inspiring? big enough to matter for years? believable?
   would it still hold if today's solution changed?
5. **Distill** to a one-line vision statement you can repeat, backed by the narrative.

## Output
A vision narrative + one-line statement, saved under `docs/` (e.g. `docs/vision.md`).
Anchors `/odeo:strategy`, `/odeo:prd`, and prioritization.

## Quality rules
- About the **customer's future**, not your feature list or your company.
- Aspirational but believable; a 2 to 5 year north star, not a quarterly goal.
- Persistent: it should not change every planning cycle. Strategy changes; vision endures.
