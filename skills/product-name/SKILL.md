# Product name

A good name is short, sayable, ownable, and evokes the value, but in a crowded space,
**ownability is the hard part**. Diverge widely, then converge on what you can actually own.

## When to use
- Naming a product, feature, or company.

## Process
1. **Anchor:** what must the name evoke (the value/positioning, from `/positioning`)? Tone + audience.
2. **Diverge:** 10 to 15 candidates across approaches, coined (Stripe/Vercel style), far-domain real word, metaphor, descriptive; in saturated spaces (AI especially) **lead with coined/oblique**, since dictionary words and on-the-nose metaphors are mostly gone. Then apply sound-craft: 1 to 2 syllables read easiest, a vowel ending feels warm, a hard consonant (k, t, v) feels strong. Don't fall for the first three.
3. **Group by approach;** shortlist on: short, sayable, spellable, evokes value, vendor-independent.
4. **Ownability check (BEFORE you attach emotionally):** search existing products/companies, especially **direct competitors in your category**; check domain + handle. Check **homophones too, not just spelling** (Rivo/Rovo, Odeo/Odoo collide by ear). A hit in a **different trademark class** may still be usable, don't discard on a bare search collision; flag same-category ones loudly. Dictionary words in crowded spaces (e.g. AI) are mostly taken.
5. **Decide on "ownable enough"** (no direct-category collision + a securable domain), not perfect global uniqueness (rarely achievable). Trademark clears by **class**, near launch.

## Output
A shortlist with rationale + availability notes. If nothing's clear, recommend a **working title** and revisit near launch (names are cheap to change pre-launch, expensive after). **Optional:** produce a copy-paste brief for an external ideation tool, what it is, who it's for, the feeling, naming criteria, hard avoids, and the names already taken.

## Example (a real lesson from this system)
```
dozens of candidates (dictionary, coined, compounds, phrases) nearly all collided in the
AI space, several by homophone (Rivo/Rovo) -> conclusion: coin/oblique, check by ear AND by
trademark class, adopt a working title, decide near launch.
```

## Quality rules
- Diverge before converging; never lead with a single name.
- Check **ownability before emotional commitment**; flag direct-competitor collisions loudly.
- "Owned enough" + a securable domain beats chasing impossible global uniqueness.
- **Honor the stop condition:** when nothing is clean after a wide sweep, commit to a working title and decide near launch, don't spiral into endless rounds.
