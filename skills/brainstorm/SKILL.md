# Brainstorm

Open the space before narrowing it. The goal is to find the *right problem and a
range of ways to solve it*, not to defend the first idea. Based on Teresa Torres's
continuous discovery (opportunity before solution) and classic diverge-then-converge.

## When to use
- A new idea, feature, or problem with no clear direction yet.
- Whenever someone (including you) jumps straight to a solution.

## Process
1. **Frame the problem, not the solution.** Ask: who hurts, how much, how often, why
   now? Restate the request as an **opportunity** ("users can't X without Y"), not a
   feature. If the user leads with a solution, surface the underlying problem first.
2. **Diverge.** Produce **7 to 10 genuinely distinct options**, not variations of one.
   Push past the obvious first three. Include at least one "do nothing / non-build"
   and one "10x ambitious" option.
3. **Group by approach.** Cluster the options (e.g. by mechanism, by user, by effort)
   so the real choices become visible.
4. **Propose selection criteria** before choosing (e.g. impact on the opportunity,
   effort, reversibility, fit with strategy, time-to-signal). Do not pick yet.
5. **Converge with the user.** Recommend, but only after the options and criteria are
   on the table. The human decides.

## Output
A grouped option set + criteria, in chat. If it firms up, feed the chosen direction
into `/prd`.

## Quality rules
- Problem before solution, always. Redirect solution-first framing.
- Distinct options, not 10 flavors of one. Quantity unlocks quality here.
- Never lead with a single answer; diverge first, then recommend.
